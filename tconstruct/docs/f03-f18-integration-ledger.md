## F07 living-entity boundary

Upstream `EntityMeltingModule.interactWithEntities` uses the structure interior, excludes the `tconstruct:melting_blacklist` entity tag and dead or invulnerable entities, and checks fire resistance. It chooses heat damage for ordinary living entities and smeltery magic damage for fire-immune entities. A matching entity recipe supplies damage and fluid; otherwise it uses damage 2 and liquid soul at `FluidValues.GLASS_PANE / 5`. It inserts fluid only after `hurt` succeeds and allows a partial tank fill. Item-entity intake #157 is a separate path and does not implement this behavior.

At the formal Horizon `02c8607ee8ebd216f2f535c1eb7b62b572830df2` target, `crates/horizon-client/src/mobs.rs` exposes `Mob::damage` and its boolean result, but `DamageKind` is a closed enum without the two TConstruct damage types. Upstream registers `tconstruct:smeltery_heat` in `minecraft:is_fire` and `tconstruct:smeltery_magic` in `minecraft:witch_resistant_to`; their identity, tags, effects and death messages are part of the damage source. The player path uses the separate registry-backed `PlayerMotion::hurt` in `crates/horizon-client/src/health.rs`. A generic, lava or ordinary magic substitute is not established as equivalent. The recipe snapshot also needs resolved entity-type tags and entity-specific output data. The missing integration is a host-supported damage source for mobs and players with those source properties and a successful-hit result, plus entity tag/recipe resolution; no new loader or generic damage approximation is assumed here.

When that integration exists, the test owner should cover a normal mob, a fire-immune mob, fire resistance, blacklist and invulnerability, an unmatched living entity, recipe-specific damage/fluid, an unsuccessful hit, a nearly full tank, and save/LAN behavior. In each case verify health, fluid quantity, and fuel against upstream, as well as unchanged item-entity intake.

## F11 guide-referenced devices

> This section records the 2026-11-02 source audit. Later registrations and candidate implementations must be checked against current main and their own verification records.

The guide-device handoff identifies three missing block/item registrations, not just missing icons. Do not ship an icon-only registration as a completed machine.

* `tconstruct:copper_gauge`: upstream `TinkerSmeltery` registers Mantle `GaugeBlock`, not `RenderingGaugeBlock`. Mantle 1.11.115 `GaugeBlock.java` uses six-way facing and an attached fluid handler on the opposite side, requires that handler for placement/survival, and displays tank 0's amount/capacity through the action bar. Unlike the obsidian rendering gauge, the copper block does not itself use `GaugeBlockEntity`. The source recipe uses copper and a colorless glass pane for four gauges. Complete registration, all six orientations, support checks and update removal, adjacent fluid reads, local and guest action-bar delivery, model and recipe together. Check empty/full/multiple fluids, all faces, removal of the support, save/reload and LAN.
* `tconstruct:seared_fluid_cannon`: source `FluidCannonBlock` and `FluidCannonBlockEntity` use a 2,000 mB tank, redstone-triggered scheduled shots and fluid-specific projectile behavior. Register its block/item and recipe together with facing, stored tank, safe container transfer, redstone timing and projectile effects. Verify amount, blocked shots, entity effects, persistence and LAN.
* `tconstruct:seared_casting_tank`: `CastingTankBlockEntity.DEFAULT_CAPACITY` is 4,000 mB with input and output slots. The lower hit region performs fluid-container interaction; the upper region manages items; redstone schedules swapping, and automation is sided. Complete registration, recipe, placement/facing, fluid and inventory transactions, redstone and automation, save/sync and display together. Verify partial capacity, full/blocked slots, failure without consumption, redstone, automation, break/drop, persistence and LAN.

An exact scan of upstream generated TConstruct recipe JSON found 20 `tconstruct:no_container` occurrences: six tool-modifier recipes, four device crafting recipes and ten melting recipes (Copper Can, Casting Tank, and seared/scorched tanks, gauges and lanterns). One `tconstruct:block_tag` occurrence is the basin water-to-mud recipe (`minecraft:convertable_to_mud`, 250 mB water, 47 ticks). This is core bundled data, not merely third-party datapack compatibility. The melting `Ingredient::parse` currently rejects both types; unsupported cases are not silently matched. The device-crafting paths have their own narrower empty-gauge checks and must be checked separately from melting and modifier matching.

The formal host `horizon-items/src/items.rs` `ItemInfo::block` supplies an item-to-block-state association. The block-tag matcher should derive each registered item's block identity from that association and the resolved block tags; it must not assume item and block IDs are identical or accept an item without a block. The stack-aware no-container matcher needs source `hasCraftingRemainingItem()` behavior, including static item remainders and the actual portable-tank/can data: no item-only check should treat filled or malformed containers as empty. The matching Melter, Foundry and GUI lookup paths must use the same result, and condition order, fluid conservation, save/reload and LAN require dedicated verification. These handlers remain unimplemented here.
